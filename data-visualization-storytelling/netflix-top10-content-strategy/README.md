# Netflix Top 10 Content Strategy Analysis

## Overview

This project uses Netflix Top 10 data to explore content popularity, viewing patterns, global reach, and how long titles remain in the rankings.

The focus is on communicating the findings through visual storytelling rather than building a predictive model.

## Analysis

The project examines:

- Viewing popularity across titles
- Differences between movies and television content
- Global reach and regional performance
- Time spent in the Top 10
- Patterns that may help explain content longevity

## Key Findings

The presentation's historical Netflix data shows that Asian-originated titles can reach many markets and remain visible across multiple weeks:

| Title | Countries with a weekly Top 10 appearance | Weeks in the Global Top 10 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Squid Game: Season 1 | 94 | 22 |
| All of Us Are Dead: Season 1 | 94 | 11 |
| ONE PIECE: Season 1 | 93 | 8 |

The Most Popular data used in the deck also lists **265.2 million first-91-day views** for Squid Game: Season 1. Views, countries reached, and weeks in the rankings are separate measures, not unique viewer counts or estimates of subscriber growth.

The recommendation is to assess content opportunities using both international reach and staying power, and to consider globally adaptable Asian-originated series as a mainstream investment category. These are findings from the data snapshot in the presentation, not current rankings or proof that a future release will achieve similar results.

The figures above are documented in the deck's global-reach chart, chart-longevity notes, and Most Popular discussion (slides 6-8).
